# Pitch, demo and project page

Presentation is 1 of 5 equally weighted criteria, and Global Awards include Best Storytelling and Art & Technology. Judges have **no context** — write for a smart outsider.

## Pick one demo format early (Sat evening)
- **Video ≤ 30 s** (hard limit — 31 s = ineligible). Bulgarian voice allowed only with English subtitles; English voice + subtitles is safer.
- **Slides ≤ 7** including title/intro, English only.
Recommendation: a 30 s screen-recorded video of the real app (shows it works → Validity) plus the same story in the README/project page.

## 30-second video skeleton (official timing from the Submission Guide)
| Time | Beat | Our content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:02 | Who | Team name (+ names in end card or description) |
| 0:02–0:05 | Hook / challenge | "On the way to Mars, your doctor is 20 minutes away." |
| 0:05–0:10 | Name + tagline | App name + one-line promise |
| 0:10–0:20 | How it works | Real screen capture: log reading → body figure turns caution → guidance → escalate to flight surgeon. Mention NASA HRP data. |
| 0:20–0:30 | Future | Wearable sensors, Mars mode, use on Earth (Antarctic stations, remote clinics) |
Write the script ≤ 70 spoken words. Burn in English subtitles. No music you don't have a licence for (use royalty-free and list it in References). No people under 18. AI voice/video → disclose and watermark.

## 7-slide deck skeleton
1. Title — app name, tagline, team name, member names, challenge name
2. Problem — long missions, no doctor nearby, slow invisible changes (RIDGE)
3. Solution — one sentence + hero screenshot
4. How it works — input → assessment (baseline + ranges) → status → guidance → escalation
5. Demo — 2–3 annotated screenshots (body figure, trend chart, night mode)
6. Science & NASA data — indicators, thresholds, HRP sources, answers to NASA's 5 questions
7. Impact & future — Mars autonomy, Earth use, next steps

## Project page texts (English) — templates
**High-level summary (≈100 words):** what we built → which challenge problem it solves → why it matters. End with the "supports self-assessment, does not diagnose" line.
**Detailed description:** What it does · How it works (assessment engine, personal baseline, escalation rules) · Design decisions and why (glanceability, colour+shape status, night mode) · Benefits · Tech stack · What we'd build next.
**NASA data:** list each NASA page/dataset used and *how* it is used in the product (e.g. "HRP Risks → mapping of indicators to hazards shown in every detail panel").
**References:** every library, font, icon set, image, 3D model, paper. Include licence.
**Use of SI:** honest list of tools and what they did (code assistance, copy drafts, image generation with watermark). Idea and direction were the team's.

## Story checks
- [ ] First 5 seconds make a non-expert care
- [ ] The word "astronaut" and the challenge name appear
- [ ] The demo shows the real app, not mock-ups only
- [ ] NASA data is named explicitly
- [ ] One memorable signature moment (body figure scan / Mars delay countdown)
- [ ] ≤ 30 s or ≤ 7 slides; English; subtitles; public link tested in private window
